# REC-1 — wiring table

The diagram `SCHEMATIC.svg` (generated by `tools/make_schematic.py`) shows the
same connections. It is a **wiring diagram**, not a verified EDA netlist — no
DRC/netlist tool has seen it.

Coordinate system of the board: the USB-C connector is at the **top** of the
board (the "back" of the device), the STEMMA QT connector at the **bottom**
(the "front"). The table below lists the pads in the order printed on the
board, top to bottom.

---

## 1. QT Py RP2040 pads used

| Silkscreen | Header | Arduino pin | GPIO | Function in REC-1 |
|------------|--------|-------------|------|-------------------|
| A2 | JP1 pad 3 | 27 | 27 | REC button input (internal pull-up, active low) |
| A3 | JP1 pad 4 | 26 | 26 | SCENE button input (internal pull-up, active low) |
| A1 | JP1 pad 2 | 28 | 28 | Record LED output (220 Ω series resistor) |
| 3V3 | JP3 pad 5 | — | — | 3.3 V rail (available on the header, not used) |
| GND | JP3 pad 6 | — | — | Common ground |
| 5V | JP3 pad 7 | — | — | 5 V from USB (not used inside the device) |
| STEMMA QT pin 1 | CONN1 | — | — | GND → OLED |
| STEMMA QT pin 2 | CONN1 | — | — | 3V3 → OLED |
| STEMMA QT pin 3 | CONN1 | SDA1 | 22 | I²C data → OLED |

Unused pads (left free): A0 (29), SDA (24), SCL (25), TX (16), RX (5),
SCK (6), MISO (4), MOSI (3).

Board pin order reference (from the official Eagle board file, for checking
against the physical board):

```
left header (JP1), top -> bottom:  A0  A1  A2  A3  SDA  SCL  TX
right header (JP3), top -> bottom: 5V  GND 3V3 MOSI MISO SCK  RX
STEMMA QT connector (CONN1):       GND  3V3  SDA1 SCL1
```

## 2. Connection list

| # | From | To | Wire | Note |
|---|------|----|------|------|
| 1 | OLED, STEMMA QT (GND, 3V3, SDA, SCL) | QT Py, STEMMA QT port | 1 × JST SH 4-pin, 100 mm (product 4210) | both ends use the order GND / 3V3 / SDA / SCL, the cable is symmetric |
| 2 | REC switch, one leg | QT Py pad A2 (GPIO27) | 22 AWG, ~60 mm | other leg to GND |
| 3 | REC switch, other leg | QT Py pad GND (JP3 pad 6) | 22 AWG, ~80 mm | common GND wire shared with the LED |
| 4 | SCENE switch, one leg | QT Py pad A3 (GPIO26) | 22 AWG, ~60 mm | other leg to GND |
| 5 | SCENE switch, other leg | GND common (see 3) | 22 AWG, ~80 mm | |
| 6 | 220 Ω resistor | QT Py pad A1 (GPIO28) | 22 AWG | series resistor, one side |
| 7 | 220 Ω resistor, other side | LED anode | 22 AWG | |

| 9 | USB-C port of the QT Py | computer | USB-C to USB-C cable (product 4199) | power **and** CDC data; the only cable to the computer |

The switches and the LED are mounted in the face panel; their wires run
through the open cavity to the board.

### 2.1 Button and LED mounting

* The two 6 mm switches are pressed into the Ø6.05 mm holes of the face
  panel (0.05 mm interference fit). They are retained by the press fit and
  the shoulder of the hole; a drop of adhesive is the fallback if a switch is
  loose.
* The LED is pressed into the Ø3.2 mm hole of the face panel.
* The 220 Ω resistor is soldered in line with the LED.

## 3. Power paths

```
USB-C (5 V) ──► QT Py LDO (AP2112K-3.3, >=500 mA) ──► 3V3 rail
                                                            ├── RP2040 core
                                                            ├── OLED module (via STEMMA QT)
                                                            ├── 220 Ω ── LED ── GND
                                                            └── internal pull-ups of the buttons
```

* The device is a bus-powered USB device. Measured design current ≈ 96 mA
  (see `REQUIREMENTS.md` §2), which is below the 100 mA that USB 2.0 allows
  without enumeration.
* Nothing else is connected to the 5 V rail; there is no battery, no radio
  and no mains input.
* The OLED is fed from **3.3 V**, which is what the module expects (it has
  its own regulator and level shifters on board).

---

## 4. Checks before closing the case

1. `+5 V` and `GND` of the QT Py are on the USB-C side; the 3V3 rail comes
   from the board's own regulator — do **not** feed 5 V into the OLED.
2. The OLED must be addressed at 0x3C (the default of the module). The
   firmware uses `Wire1` (I2C1) because that is the bus of the STEMMA QT
   connector on this board.
3. Every switch has exactly two electrical connections (the Omron B3F-1000
   has four legs, two of them connected internally per side — check with a
   meter before soldering).
4. The LED polarity: long leg = anode = to the resistor, short leg = cathode
   = GND.
5. Measure the current between the USB-C plug and the board when the OLED is
   lit and the LED is on: it must stay below 100 mA.
